# Typed Web Commons Charter

**Status:** Provisional Genesis charter 0.1  
**Applies from:** 2026-08-10

## Mission

Create an open, interoperable, provenance-preserving protocol that transforms web representations into typed interfaces for software agents while protecting human sovereignty, publisher agency, semantic plurality, and independent implementation.

## Public assets

The following are held for the commons:

- protocol specifications and RFCs;
- canonical schemas and encodings;
- conformance tests and public vectors;
- provenance and trust models;
- core semantic kernel and public ontology modules;
- reference implementations and SDKs released under their declared open licenses;
- public registry metadata and compatibility reports;
- project governance and treasury records.

No donor, maintainer, company, model provider, cloud provider, or chain purchases ownership of these assets.

## Founding laws

1. **Source-statement fidelity.** Canonical results identify what an origin represented and the exact derivation applied.
2. **Native meaning first.** Normalization never erases the source-native statement.
3. **No invented certainty.** Unsupported knowledge is unresolved, not fabricated.
4. **Separate dimensions.** Integrity, publisher identity, authority, confidence, freshness, and evidence completeness remain distinct.
5. **Visible failure.** Degradation, drift, revocation, contradiction, and uncertainty are explicit states.
6. **Language sovereignty.** Specifications and conformance—not an implementation language—define the protocol.
7. **Local and cloud neutrality.** Essential protocol functions remain self-hostable.
8. **Open extension, governed canon.** Anyone may publish extensions; canonization requires transparent admission.
9. **Least authority.** Read access is the default; actions require scoped capabilities and stronger gates.
10. **Optional cryptographic infrastructure.** Chains may notarize or settle but are never mandatory protocol sovereigns.
11. **No token.** Governance rights and protocol participation are not sold through a speculative asset.
12. **Honest sustenance.** Maintainer compensation is legitimate, disclosed, and never exchanged for ownership of the standard.

## Genesis governance

During Genesis, the founder is the final decision-maker and steward of release scope. This is practical responsibility, not private ownership of the protocol.

Material protocol decisions must be recorded through an RFC or architecture decision record containing:

- the problem;
- alternatives considered;
- security and compatibility effects;
- rationale;
- decision owner;
- date and version;
- reversal or migration conditions.

Funding does not purchase technical votes. Contributors earn influence through sustained, trustworthy work and review responsibility.

A technical steering structure becomes appropriate only after there are several independent maintainers or implementations. Governance must not simulate decentralization before a real community exists.

## Publisher and human rights

The project will not normalize bypassing access controls, paywalls, authentication, rate limits, or anti-abuse systems. It will provide mechanisms for verified publishers to review, approve, correct, sign, and revoke interfaces associated with their origins.

No write, payment, destructive, or legally consequential operation may be inferred into production authority without an explicit capability model and separate security gate.

## Financial governance

All project-controlled wallets and accounts must be declared publicly. Public ledgers record incoming and outgoing project funds. Maintainer runway is reported as compensation or a grant, not disguised as an infrastructure invoice. Project receipts are published in redacted form when safe; originals are retained privately for accounting and verification.

Contributions confer no equity, ownership, guaranteed service, private protocol access, or governance entitlement unless a separate written agreement explicitly describes a service procurement that remains compatible with this charter.

## Amendment

Genesis charter amendments require a public change record explaining why the amendment strengthens or preserves the mission. No amendment may privatize the protocol, retroactively conceal treasury activity, or convert financial sponsorship into ownership of technical truth.

## Succession and dissolution

No indispensable credential, repository, domain, wallet, signing key, or production system should remain permanently controlled by one person.

When the project has additional trusted stewards, critical assets should migrate to threshold or multisignature control with documented recovery procedures.

If the steward organization dissolves, public protocol assets should transfer to a compatible open-source or public-interest steward rather than a private buyer.
